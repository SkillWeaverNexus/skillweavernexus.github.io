Combat Awareness and Cover

Combat Awareness¶

In combat, all sufficiently intelligent creatures have an awareness of where all detectable combatants on the map are, or their last detected location. For creatures relying on sight, this means all visible creatures not behind cover, and an awareness of the last known location of creatures that become invisible or dart behind solid cover. When a creature or object moves at least 20ft (4 grid spaces) in a straight line while out of sight to enemy combatants, their position becomes unknown.

Only creatures with known location can be directly targeted by actions like attacks or spells; targets with unknown locations can still be impacted by area affects, potentially revealing their position.

When a creature whose position is unknown becomes revealed, either by becoming visible, moving into line of sight, or making an attack, they gain the Protected status until they move 10 ft or end their turn. This status prevents them from being targeted by reactions. When a creature is known to be associated with a Cover Area (A continuous section of cover (like a wall, hedge, or line of columns) up to 20ft long is considered one “Cover Area”), that cover area no longer grants protected status, as other combatants have an eye to that location. A combatant must find a different cover area if they which to re-attack with Protected status. Larger Contiguous Areas (like a 50ft wall) can count as multiple Cover Areas; a GM can delineate what regions might reasonably be considered separate. The inside and outside of the same cover (like opposite sides of a wall) count as separate Cover Areas, if the scenario supports it.

Cover¶

Cover is divided into three categories:

  1. half cover, which imposes minor disadvantage on attacks
  2. 3/4 cover which imposes major disadvantage on attacks
  3. Full cover, with which individuals are not directly target-able.

When utilizing physical cover as full cover, the terrain determines if you need to crouch/duck down or otherwise adjust yourself to achieve full cover: in cases where this is required, changing your position from visible/half/three-quarter cover to full cover expends 5 feet of movement, as does changing your position from full cover to visible/half/three quarter cover.

When attempting to combat from behind partial cover, you must make a DL 15 Investigation check as a free action to find a suitable alignment that doesn’t disadvantage your own view; failing this check, the disadvantage that applies to others targeting you applies to you targeting others as well, though this may cancel out with advantages you gain from being elevated, etc. So long as you don’t move, a successful check continues to apply to your location.

Example

As an archer, you have found a gallery level to the temple your party is fighting in, which has a low wall and provides up to full cover from the combatants below. Your elevation is providing you with minor advantage to hit and a +2 bonus to damage. You’ve snuck along the wall under total cover for 20 ft, and your position is unknown to those below.

On your turn, you expend 5ft of movement to stand up to see over the wall, but fail your investigation roll to shoot while hiding. However, you do have the Protected status, meaning no one can use reactions to attack you. You make your attacks with a straight roll (cover disadvantage canceling elevation advantage), then expend 5 ft of movement to duck back down under full cover.

When you end your turn, your position becomes known. Let’s say you choose to stay in the same spot to attack on your next turn, for some reason. Things are very different now. An enemy combatant sees you rising and calls out a reaction shot against you. Under the reaction rules, this proceeds when you finish your movement of standing up, so you may have an arrow or two incoming before you get a chance to attack. Once you are past that, though, you can proceed with your turn as normal. Next turn, you may try to sneak somewhere not behind this wall, as it is a contiguous Cover Area and will no longer grant Protected status.

Invisibility¶

Unless otherwise noted, spells cast from invisibility still reveal the location of the caster, and ranged projectiles (e.g., arrows, throwing stars) become visible when they leave the invisible individual’s vicinity. In both cases, such attacks reveal the location of the attacker, even if they are invisible (in general, attacking does not drop invisibility, though most invisibilities drop when you take damage).

Invisibility is considered 3/4 cover if your position is known; turning invisible via a spell or ability does not cost movement. When you turn invisible, your position is known until you have moved 20 ft, as detailed in the rules above.
