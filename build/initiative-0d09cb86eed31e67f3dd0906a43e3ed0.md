# Initiative

Skillweaver does not use a fixed-turn initiative system that involves all individuals rolling. Instead, "initiative" is granted per **group of allied individuals**. Under typical circumstances where neither party is surprised, a nominated member of each party of combatants makes a {term}`Fate Roll` to see which party gets the jump. This fate roll is a bit unique in that it has no table: it is simply a head to  head across the involved parties.

:::{note} Tie Rolls
In the case of a tie, the party with a greater number of individuals goes first; if this does not break the tie, then the head to head is re-rolled to resolve that order.
:::

The parties then act in order based on the outcome of that roll. For 2 parties going head to head, they would thus alternate back and forth from this point, starting with the winner of the check. For 3+ groups fighting at once, the first action by each group is taken in turn and then continues to cycle. 

The second turn of every round is special, and is called the {term}`Environmental Round`. Lair actions or persistent weather effects or collapsing dungeons are all examples of environmental effects that can affect combat, and are resolved or progress during this turn in each round. This turn comes second in each round regardless of initiative, which "skips" this turn.

When it is your group’s turn to act, anyone within your group can choose to take their turn. It is up to you to strategize and decide who has the best move prepared or is setting up for a combination move later. Every individual gets 1 turn per round of combat, but as is discussed in the section on [Reactions](reactions), you have plenty of opportunity to make a difference outside of your turn.

## Multiplied Presence

Some enemies and monsters have a special property known as multiplied presence. This represents their enhanced power as individual beings which can take on multiple attackers at once. Enemies with the multiplied presence trait have a certain number of turns in combat greater than 1. These creatures gain a set number of points of stamina to actively use at the start of each of their turns within a round, but have only 6 points of stamina total to use for defense or reactionary moves throughout the round.  Turns are taken back and forth between the enemy and the party until the enemy is out of multiplied presences. If there is more than one remaining ally, they take their turns in succession to finish the round. Single, major monsters or enemy bosses are likely to have this trait.
