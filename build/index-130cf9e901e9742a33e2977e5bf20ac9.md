# Welcome to SkillWeaver Nexus



Skillweaver Nexus (SWN) is a medium-weight TTRPG designed to facilitate dynamic, kinetic gameplay. In this section, we will summarize a few of the mechanics of the system, and the motivations for the overall design structure. We call SWN a ``medium-weight'' RPG because it uses many semi-crunchy mechanics, character options, and combat systems found int full-bore RPGs like D\&D or Pathfinder, but in other ways is simpler than these systems, particularly when it comes to races, classes, and assertions about the in-game universe, pantheon, or other ancillary points. 

This chapter is designed to introduce many of the mechanics that make SWN what it is; several are expanded upon more fully in later chapters. The idea here is to summarize the system at a high level.

The name of the system references the method in which you build up a character --- rather than choosing a class and subclass at the start of a campaign, which then determine your player progression and provide abilities and features at different player levels with little further decision making, SWN characters grow via the selection of abilities on a series of skill trees. 

Furthermore, new abilities are not only obtained when you level up as a player. SWN is designed such that your game master (GM) can award \textbf{skill points} every session, either for heroic actions, successes, or roleplay choices, or for general milestones. In each session, you can expect to earn 1-3 skill points, which you can use (or save) toward abilities in the skill trees. 

Your character levels up for every 10 skill points obtained, and this can impact which tier of abilities you can take. But you can also expect to choose, and gain, a new ability of some kind nearly every session. 

By "weaving your skills" in this way, you can dynamically create a character over time, learning new skills most relevant to your adventure and goals



```{note}
Just as SWN is fast-paced and dynamic about obtaining skills, it is also dynamic about loosing them. You can unlearn some abilities later on in order to recoup skill points to spend, much like your character letting skills lapse if they move in a different direction.
```
