# Combat Awareness and Cover

## Combat Awareness
In combat, all sufficiently intelligent creatures have an awareness of where all detectable combatants on the map are, or their last detected location. For creatures relying on sight, this means all visible creatures not behind cover, and an awareness of the last known location of creatures that become invisible or dart behind solid cover. When a creature or object moves at least 20ft (4 grid spaces) in a straight line while out of sight to enemy combatants, their position becomes unknown. 

Only creatures with known location can be directly targeted by actions like attacks or spells; targets with unknown locations can still be impacted by area affects, potentially revealing their position. 

When a creature whose position is unknown becomes revealed, either by becoming visible, moving into line of sight, or making an attack, they gain the Protected status until they move 10 ft or end their turn. This status prevents them from being targeted by reactions. When a creature is known to be associated with a {term}`Cover Area` (A continuous section of cover (like a wall, hedge, or line of columns) up to 20ft long is considered one "Cover Area"), that cover area no longer grants protected status, as other combatants have an eye to that location. A combatant must find a different cover area if they which to re-attack with Protected status. Larger Contiguous Areas (like a 50ft wall) can count as multiple Cover Areas; a GM can delineate what regions might reasonably be considered separate. The inside and outside of the same cover (like opposite sides of a wall) count as separate Cover Areas, if the scenario supports it.


## Cover 

Cover is divided into three categories:
1. half cover, which imposes minor disadvantage on attacks 
2. 3/4 cover which imposes major disadvantage on attacks
3. Full cover, with which individuals are not directly target-able.

When utilizing physical cover as full cover, the terrain determines if you need to crouch/duck down or otherwise adjust yourself to achieve full cover: in cases where this is required, changing your position from visible/half/three-quarter cover to full cover expends 5 feet of movement, as does changing your position from full cover to visible/half/three quarter cover. 

When attempting to combat from behind partial cover, you must make a DL 15 Investigation check as a free action to find a suitable alignment that doesn't disadvantage your own view; failing this check, the disadvantage that applies to others targeting you applies to you targeting others as well, though this may cancel out with advantages you gain from being elevated, etc.

## Invisibility

Unless otherwise noted, spells cast from invisibility still reveal the location of the caster, and ranged projectiles (e.g., arrows, throwing stars) become visible when they leave the invisible individual's vicinity. In both cases, such attacks reveal the location of the attacker, even if they are invisible (in general, attacking does not drop invisibility, though most invisibilities drop when you take damage).

Invisibility is considered 3/4 cover if your position is known; turning invisible via a spell or ability does not cost movement. When you turn invisible, your position is known until you have moved 20 ft, as detailed in the rules above. 



:::{note} Example

Your party is fighting in a temple, and an enemy archer is hiding in the rafters above, taking pot shots. You see them duck down and out of site, defining your last known position of them. 

If they pop up 20 ft down the rafters, you don't have time to both clock their appearance and line up a reactionary shot/cast; they use their grace period to take a shot and duck down.
