# Reactions

A reaction in combat is a **single combat action** that you take outside of your turn. You can trigger your reaction at any time, including in the middle of another character's turn. You can use your reaction either to 

- Expend 1 stamina to move up to half your movement distance, or
- Expend 1 stamina to use one of your other available actions. That action still costs its normal stamina amount.

Note that you cannot do both movement and actions in the same reaction.

The flexibility to invoke reactions is another reason to closely watch combat as it unfolds.

## Examples

Here are a few cases to illustrate the use of reactions in combat:

- An enemy pops out from behind a column to take a longbow shot at your companion. As the party's ranged member, you call your reaction to shoot back at them before they can pop back into hiding. Once their attack is resolved, but before they move or attack again, you resolve your own attack on them. 
- Your team has set an ambush on an enemy troupe entering a cave. During the combat, your job is to shoot a rope holding up a cage at just the right moment. You keep watch during combat, and when the final enemy begins moving into region on the ground covered by the cage, you call your reaction shot. When that enemy finishes their movement, your slicing of the rope proceeds. 
- The "Arcane Redirection" spell specifies directly that it interrupts the process of another spell being cast. When used as a reaction, this spell details that a roll is made to attempt to redirect a spell being cast toward another target. Thus, if you call this reaction when another is describing some particular spell, it resolves before the spell effect as intended takes place. On the other hand, if your reaction was to cast a retaliatory attack spell against the caster, that would happen just after their own spell effect takes place.


This dynamic format for reactions is designed to allow for more engaging combat, but it can also be abused. Your GM has the final word on where a reaction slots in, and all players are encouraged to be respectful about taking reactions (particularly on another player's turn). One system might be to raise a hand or token if you plan to take a reaction at the next available opportunity.
