# Stealth 



Stealth, for being a somewhat niche topic generally, is of the utmost importance to most RPG adventures, with a large amount of sneaking around going on. We thus choose to elevate stealth to a core stat, allowing us to explore more of the subtlety and variety that stealthy encounters require. 

There are 5 subdivisions to stealth, which define different ``modes'' of being stealthy. These are touch, sight, sound, smell, and social, and checks of these type are denoted as, e.g., Stealth/Touch. You may notice the absence of taste. We hope you are not in any scenarios where you have to be stealthy to avoid being detected by someone's tastebuds. 

What is a stealth check? In SWN, a stealth check is one made to avoid detection, where detection is understood to be ``noticed in an undesired way.'' Below, we lay out some scenarios that might apply to the different forms of stealth check. By default, you will roll your stealth die in order to make a stealth check, regardless of the mode. But many abilities your player might have can provide circumstantial bonuses to a subset of modes, meaning you might be better at pickpocketing (Stealth/Touch) than being quiet (Stealth/Sound). Sometimes, a situation may require more than one form of Stealth (e.g., being quiet and unseen). In these cases, the GM should generally for a given moment or stage, choose whichever check is the most relevant, with the assumption that each blends slightly into the other. 

## Touch 

Stealth/Touch primarily concerns tasks similar to pickpocketing: the art of touching or nearly touching something or someone without being detected. Some examples might include 

- Stealing a key of a guard's belt.
- Slipping something into a noble's pocket.
- Extracting a golden egg from within a sleeping dragon's clutches.


## Sight 
The ``default'' mode, Stealth/Sight is a check made to avoid being seen. Examples might include

- Melting into the shadows to avoid the sight of a guard.
- Freezing in the underbrush as a dragon flies overhead.


## Sound 
Stealth/Sound is a check made to avoid being heard. For example, if a party is sneaking through a guarded castle at night, they would likely make a Stealth/Sound check when passing through hallways without a guard in them (but which might have guards just around the corner or behind closed doors). When entering a hallway with said guards, the group would then make a Stealth/Sight check. Some other examples include

- Avoiding a creature that relies primarily on sound (a la Clickers in The Last of Us).
- Avoiding waking up a slumbering beast, 
- Muttering an incantation without someone nearby hearing.


Sometimes, evading being heard means avoiding being heard as you. For example, If you dove into the back of a cart, but there isn't enough straw to cover you and the guards walking through are tall enough to peer into the cart, you might ask your DM to make a Stealth/Sound check to initiate a snuffling pig, convincing the guard there is no need to come closer. Distracting sounds may also apply; throwing a pebble far from your location to mask your own sounds would be a valid example of a Stealth/Sound check. 

## Smell 

Perhaps the least conventional, Stealth/Smell checks arise more often with monsters that rely primarily on smell --- but can have other applications... for example, 

- You fell in some mud / dung and are now trying to sneak past some guards, who may not see or hear you but may well smell you.
- You are stealing some stinky cheese, and don't have anything to mask the smell with.


## Social 

This form of stealth check is somewhat unique from the others because it invokes the part of detection that is ``unwanted'' --- that is, it allows you to be seen, heard, etc., without drawing attention. The classic cases for this are, e.g., 

- Blending into a crowd.
- Picking a lock on an open street while looking like you are jiggling your own, very real, keys. 
- Masking your intentions or guilt when talking.


Note that Stealth/Social roles, like all roles, are situational. If you are being chased by city guards and dive into a crowded marketplace, a successful Stealth/Social role doesn't mean a guard looking right at you won't see you, because they know what you look like. On the other hand, a successful Stealth/Social role might prevent the crowd around you from reacting to your presence in a way that alerts the guards. If you can buy enough time, you may be able to put on a disguise. From here, you have a chance at Stealth/Sight checks, or a higher but manageable-DL Stealth/Social check to pass through the the line of sight of a guard without notice.
