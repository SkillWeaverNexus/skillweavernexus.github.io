# Learning Magic

SWN defines three core pathways through which a character might learn magical abilities: 

- Scholarship, in which the study of the binding rules and formulae of arcana are used to craft and manipulate magic, 
- Resonance, in which meditation with nature and spiritual examination of naturally-occurring arcana allow one to emulate those effects, and
- Gifted, in which some spirit, entity, god, etc., elects to grant a particular magical spell. 


Your build, backstory, and elements of the setting you are playing in will determine which of these paths is relevant to your character (and which are possible). When you take the "Arcane Initiate" ability, which unlocks spellcasting, you will choose which single ability is associated with your magic casting. Some Origin Tier Abilities dictate which ability is to be used for your casting, if you choose to be a caster while having that particular origin. For example, the "Scholist" Origin in the Reasoning Tree dictates that your arcane ability die is Arcana. 

## Intentional Learning

If you have spent a Skill Point to learn a new spell via the Reasoning Tree, you have the opportunity to spend {term}`Downtime` learning a spell. Depending on the spell's strength, your GM will select a DL associated with learning that spell. During your Downtime, you can make an ability check towards that total DL (so some spells might take more than one downtime to learn). 

Which check you use depends on how your character interacts with magic: 

- A Scholarly character would make Arcana checks,
- A Resonance character would make Nature checks,
- A Gifted character of a god or established faith would make a Religion check, characterizing their communion, and 
- A Gifted character of some entity might make a Persuasion, Intimidation, or Deception check to entreaty for more power --- you would work this out with your GM before playing.


Upon reaching the DL for the spell, you have incorporated it into your understanding. In SWN, all spells you know are accessible to you at all times, meaning you do not prepare or switch out spells. 

## Discovering Arcana

Adventurers are also like to stumble upon examples of arcana in the world around them. These provide opportunities for learning additional spells without spending downtime working them out. 

Examples of these "in-world" hints at magic might be 

- scribbled notes of a mad wizard in an abandoned tower,
- a naturally-occurring glowing globule, or
- a religious artifact laced with power.


In these scenarios, you have one chance to learn some latent magic. You make your relevant ability roll for your character, with the DL set to your die in the relevant skill plus the spell's toxicity (see next section). For example, if you are a scholist, and attempt to decipher the mad wizard's scrawling, you would make an Arcana check. If your Reasoning die was a d10, and the spell had a toxicity of 3, your DL is 10+3=13. 

If you succeed, you instantly learn the spell. When learning magic in this way, you do not need to expend Skill Points on Arcana slots.
