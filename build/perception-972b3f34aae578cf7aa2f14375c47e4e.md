# Perception 

Perception governs the senses and the act of noticing or identifying things in your surroundings. While other core skills may be more active or expressive, Perception governs reception — what your character can detect or deduce about the world through their senses.

## Sight
The default mode of perception, concerning things seen with the naked eye (or enhanced vision). Used when attempting to spot a visible detail or hazard.

- Noticing a strange marking on a wall across the room.
- Seeing a shape in the fog that isn’t quite right.
- Spotting a tower in the distance.~


## Sound
When a character is relying on what they can hear. Often used in darkness, or when sight is obscured.

- Hearing footsteps in a tunnel ahead.
- Detecting whispered conversation behind a closed door.
- Recognizing a familiar pattern of knocking or bells.


## Smell
Generally circumstantial, but useful when scent is an active narrative or environmental factor.

- Smelling sulfur before an elemental appears.
- Identifying a particular perfume, or a trail of blood.
- Detecting the presence of gas, decay, or rot.




## Tracking
The ability to follow trails, identify movement patterns, or simply understand the layout of natural surroundings.

- Following hoofprints through muddy ground.
- Deducing the path taken by a thief across rooftops.
- Telling how long ago a campfire was extinguished.
- Inferring the layout of complicated, unlabeled streets in a foreign city.


## Vigilance
Vigilance is the Endurance of Perception --- it encompasses tasks that involve keeping a steady, focused attention on something, noticing the moment things change. Some classic examples include

- Keeping watch while the party rests at night.
- Waiting for the subtle twitch of movement behind a curtain.
- Standing at a crowd's edge, eyes roving for anything suspicious.
- Watching the players at a card game to identify cheaters or card counters.
