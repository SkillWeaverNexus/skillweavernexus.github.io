# Defense

When an enemy of some kind chooses to attack you, you have multiple options for how to defend yourself. If an attack roll against you is successful, you have three options for how to defend:

- Take it: absorb the damage. [0 stamina]
- Parry it: deflect half of the damage [1 stamina]
- Dodge it: evade the damage entirely [2 stamina]

Remembering that you have 6 stamina to use throughout your turn, choosing to take damage (or parry) might preserve stamina to use in your own attacks and reactions. Armor, discussed below, is oriented around negating some damage at 0 stamina cost (but with increased costs to parry and dodge). Note that if an attack misses you, you do not have to expend stamina attempting a form of defense. 

```{note}
The rules for defense apply to psychic/mental or internal/magic attacks as well as physical ones. A psychic lance can be parried or dodged by virtue of a steeling of emptying of the mind. Psychic attacks do bypass most armor, though some equipment exists to shield against such attacks.
```

## Armor 

Armor negates a certain portion of incoming damage as defined by the item. Better armor negates more damage, of more types, and with less of a penalty on other aspects of your combat (such as movement or parry/dodge ability).

Take the simple, ubiquitous `Guardsman's Armor`:

:::{dropdown} Guardsman's Armor (Heavy Armor)
:open:
- WR: Physicality d8 
- Damage Reduction: 4 (bludgeoning), 2 (slashing,piercing) [nonmagical]
- Parry: +1 stamina 
- Dodge: +2 stamina 
- Movement: reduced by 5ft
:::

In order to wear this armor set, you must have at least a `d8` in your Physicality {term}`Core Skill`. While wearing it, it negates the first 4 points of nonmagical bludgeoning damage (and first 2 points of nonmagical slashing or piercing damage) from any attack you take. But it's heavy, and parrying while wearing it costs a total of 2 stamina, while dodging is extremely stamina draining (4 stamina to dodge). And on top of that, it reduces your overall movement speed by 5 ft. 

For a level 1-2 character for whom incoming attacks might, on average, be 2-6 points of damage, this armor is still an attractive option, particularly if you plan to be a bit tanky and focus your stamina on swinging a big sword about. That said, this is about as mundane (read: bad) as armor gets. A high level adventurer in an ancient tomb may stumble upon much better items, e.g., 

:::{dropdown} Melphaerin's Helm (Very Rare Medium Armor)
:open:
- WR: Physicality d12 
- Damage Reduction: 8 (bludgeoning), 10 (slashing,piercing) [incl. magical]
- Parry: +0 stamina 
- Dodge: +2 stamina 

**Melphaerin's Light [1 TOX]**: When activated, the helm shines in a bright, 30ft radius, and all undead within the region take 2d4 radiant damage and become frightened. Once used, it cannot be used again until you have completed a full rest.
:::


This helm (which as an individual armor piece can be combined with others) gathers up much more damage, still lets you parry for 1 stamina total, adds magical damage types, and has a 1 TOX ability the bearer can use. You do need a d12 in Physicality to wear it, though. As you proceed through combat, if incoming damage is close to 8 or 10, you can save the most stamina by letting the armor absorb it. If damage is rolling in higher than this, you may choose to mix things up, taking it some of the time, but parrying or dodging at others. (Parrying would make sense if incoming damage exceeded 16/20 points, for which halving it would do more than the armor).
